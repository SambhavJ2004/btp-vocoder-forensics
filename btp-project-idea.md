# Does Better-Sounding Mean Harder-to-Detect?

### Measuring the relationship between vocoder quality and audio deepfake detectability

**B.Tech Project — Netaji Subhas University of Technology**
Team of 3 · Draft v1 · September 2026

---

## Part 1 — The idea, in plain terms

### The situation

AI systems can now generate speech that sounds convincingly human. The component that does the final step of this — turning a compressed representation of sound into an actual audio waveform — is called a **vocoder**. Modern vocoders like BigVGAN and SpecDiff-GAN are GAN-based, and they are very good.

Separately, there are **detectors**: systems trained to tell whether a piece of audio was generated or genuinely recorded. They work by spotting small, consistent mistakes that generators leave behind. Every generator leaves traces.

### The assumption nobody has tested

The field generally assumes these two things move together: as vocoders get better at sounding human, they automatically become harder to detect.

This sounds obvious. It might be wrong.

Sounding real to a **human ear** and looking real to a **forensic classifier** are two different tests. A vocoder could produce audio that a listener finds flawless while still leaving a machine-visible signature that a detector picks up instantly. The reverse is also possible: audio that sounds slightly off but is forensically clean.

Nobody has measured whether these two properties are actually linked.

### What we are doing

Take six vocoders spanning a wide range of quality, from an old non-neural method to state-of-the-art BigVGAN. Have each one regenerate the *same* set of real speech recordings.

For each vocoder, measure two independent numbers:

1. **How good does it sound?** (a perceptual quality score)
2. **How easily is it caught?** (a detection error rate)

Then plot one against the other.

If the points form a clean upward trend, we have quantified the tradeoff and put a number on it for the first time. If they don't, we have shown that audio quality and detectability are **independent properties**, which contradicts the field's working assumption and has a direct practical implication: someone could deliberately build a vocoder that is undetectable without it needing to sound especially good.

Either outcome is a result. There is no version of this experiment that produces nothing.

### Why this design works

The critical trick is that every vocoder regenerates the *same original recordings*. Same words, same speaker, same background. The only thing that changes between conditions is which vocoder did the work.

That means any difference we measure can only be caused by the vocoder. This turns the study from a survey into a controlled experiment, and it is what makes the conclusion defensible.

## Part 2 — Technical formulation

### Research question

> Are perceptual quality and forensic detectability orthogonal properties of a neural vocoder?

Vocoder research optimises exclusively for perceptual quality. No mainstream vocoder is trained to evade spoofing detection. The implicit assumption that improvements in the former produce improvements in the latter has not, to our knowledge, been systematically quantified.

### Why vocoder artifacts exist

Mel-spectrogram extraction is lossy and non-invertible in two distinct ways:

1. **Phase is discarded entirely.** Only magnitude is retained.
2. **Frequency resolution is compressed.** Roughly 1024 STFT bins are folded into 80–100 perceptually-weighted mel bands, with the heaviest compression at high frequencies.

Consequently, mel → waveform is an **ill-posed inverse problem**: infinitely many waveforms are consistent with a given mel-spectrogram. The vocoder cannot recover the true signal, so it selects one using a prior learned from its training distribution.

The resulting reconstruction error is therefore **systematic, not stochastic**. It is determined by architecture, training data, and loss function, and it recurs consistently across utterances. Systematic error is structured; structured error is learnable; learnable error is detectable.

This also predicts *where* artifacts concentrate:

- **Phase** is synthesised from nothing, so phase-coherence errors are a strong forensic cue.
- **High frequencies** are the most heavily compressed in the mel representation, so the vocoder has the least information there and must hallucinate the most.

The second point connects directly to BigVGAN's design. Its anti-aliasing machinery exists to suppress high-frequency fold-back artifacts generated by its own nonlinearities, which is precisely the region detectors rely on most.

### Phase A — Controlled resynthesis

Construct a dataset in which **vocoder identity is the only free variable**.

- **Source corpus:** LJSpeech subset (~3,000 utterances), extended to VCTK for speaker diversity if time allows.
- **Method:** resynthesis, not text-to-speech. Real audio → mel-spectrogram → vocoder → waveform. This preserves content, speaker and prosody across conditions, and provides a paired reference that enables intrusive quality metrics.
- **Vocoder ladder (approx. worst to best):** Griffin-Lim, MelGAN, HiFi-GAN v1, SpecDiff-GAN, BigVGAN-base, BigVGAN (112M).

**Confound controls.** These are invariants, not preferences. Violating any one of them silently invalidates the study.

| Confound | Control |
|---|---|
| Sample-rate mismatch | Resample every output to 16 kHz mono, exactly once. Never resample twice. |
| Mel configuration differences | Cannot be fully eliminated across vocoders; document per-condition and discuss as a limitation. |
| Silence duration | Prior work on ASVspoof 2019 LA showed detectors partially keying on leading/trailing silence rather than spoofing artifacts. Apply identical trimming to real and resynthesized audio. |
| Loudness | Identical normalisation target across all conditions. |
| Encoding | Identical format and bit depth throughout. |

### Phase B — Independent measurement of both axes

**Axis 1: perceptual quality.**

- **UTMOS** — learned MOS predictor, primary metric (human listening tests are out of scope for a BTP budget).
- **PESQ**, **mel-cepstral distortion (MCD)** — intrusive metrics, available because resynthesis provides a paired reference.
- **F0 / periodicity error** — targets pitch-specific artifacts.

**Axis 2: forensic detectability.**

Reported as **Equal Error Rate (EER)**, the operating point at which false-acceptance and false-rejection rates coincide. EER is used rather than accuracy because it is threshold-free and independent of the fake-to-real ratio in the evaluation set, making it comparable across systems. ASVspoof additionally reports cost-based metrics from the t-DCF family, which weight error types by downstream impact on speaker verification.

Two protocols are run, because they measure **different quantities**:

- **Mismatched (zero-shot).** A detector trained on ASVspoof 2019 LA is evaluated on our vocoders without adaptation. Measures *generalisation failure of deployed systems*. Practical relevance.
- **Matched.** A detector is trained specifically on real-vs-vocoder-X. Measures *how much artifact information the signal contains at all*, independent of any single detector's blind spots. Serves as an upper bound on separability. Scientific relevance.

Conflating these two is a recurring weakness in the literature. Separating them cleanly is a low-cost contribution in itself.

**Detectors:** AASIST (~300k parameters, trains on a single T4), RawNet2, and a frozen wav2vec2 frontend with a trainable AASIST backend where compute permits.

**Headline analysis:** Spearman rank correlation between the quality metric and matched EER across the vocoder set.

| Outcome | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Strong positive correlation | Quality and detectability are coupled. The field's assumption holds, and we have quantified the exchange rate. |
| Weak or absent correlation | The two are orthogonal. Perceptual optimisation and forensic evasion are independent objectives, implying an undetectable vocoder need not be a high-quality one. |

**Mechanism ablation — band-limited detection.** Apply low-pass filtering at varying cutoffs, retrain the detector on each band-limited variant, and identify where detection performance collapses. If detection of an older vocoder degrades sharply once content above ~8 kHz is removed while BigVGAN detection is unaffected, the artifact is localised to the high band and the anti-aliasing effect is demonstrated directly. This converts a correlation into a mechanism.

### Phase C — Source tracing (extension)

Reframe from binary detection to **attribution**: which vocoder produced this audio?

- **Closed-set.** N-way classification over the known vocoder set. Expected to perform well; included as a baseline.
- **Open-set.** Under a leave-one-vocoder-out protocol, the system encounters a generator absent from training. Softmax cannot express "none of these" and will assign a confident incorrect label. Requires score-based rejection: energy-based OOD scoring or calibrated distance thresholds in embedding space.
- **Family-level hypothesis.** Do GAN vocoders form a coherent cluster in embedding space, distinct from non-GAN approaches? If so, family-level attribution may generalise to unseen family members even where instance-level attribution fails.

### Where the novelty sits

Not in a new detector and not in a new vocoder. The contribution is **experimental design**:

1. Paired resynthesis enabling intrusive quality metrics and detectability on identical content.
2. Clean separation of matched and mismatched detection protocols.
3. A band-limited ablation that identifies the artifact mechanism rather than stopping at correlation.

Every claim traces back to a controlled comparison, which is what makes it defensible under examination.

## Part 3 — Execution

### Team split

Each member owns a distinct thesis chapter.

| Role | Ownership |
|---|---|
| **Person A** | Data and vocoder pipeline. Checkpoint sourcing, resynthesis, confound enforcement, dataset publication. |
| **Person B** | Quality measurement. UTMOS, PESQ, MCD, F0 error. Band-wise spectral analysis. Owns the X-axis. |
| **Person C** | Detection. AASIST/RawNet2 setup, both protocols, Phase C source tracing. Owns the Y-axis. |

**Dependency risk.** B and C both consume A's output. To avoid two members idling, A ships a deliberately throwaway **v0 dataset** in week two — 200 utterances, Griffin-Lim and HiFi-GAN only — purely so B and C can build and debug their pipelines in parallel. The full dataset later replaces it by changing a mount path.

### Compute

Kaggle free tier: approximately 30 GPU-hours per week per account, P100 (16 GB) or dual T4, 12-hour session cap, 20 GB persistent storage. Three accounts give the team roughly 90 GPU-hours weekly, which is sufficient. Phase A is inference-only; detector training is the only significant cost.

Notes:
- Prefer T4 over P100. The P100 is CUDA compute capability 6.0 and recent PyTorch builds require 7.0+.
- Do not attempt to install all vocoders in one environment; dependency trees conflict. Run each in an isolated session and persist output to a Kaggle Dataset.
- Code lives in a GitHub repository; Kaggle notebooks act as thin drivers. This also provides the reproducibility section for the thesis.
